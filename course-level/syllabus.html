Welcome to Elementary Latin

This course is a first, joyful encounter with the Roman world and its language. Your student will learn Roman numerals, meet the gods of Rome, discover why October doesn't mean "eighth" the way it sounds like it should, and start noticing Latin hiding inside everyday English words. There is no grammar drilling in this course — that begins in 6th grade. This year is about curiosity, vocabulary, and falling in love with a very old and very present language.

🏛️ This course meets 80 minutes a week, in two ~40-minute sessions (the same weekly allocation as elementary Art and Music). All content is delivered through Canvas; there is no live class session required.

Two Lanes, One Course

Every module covers the same weekly topic, but is delivered two ways so it fits how your student learns at their age:

LaneGradesWhat it looks like
FoundationsK-2Picture-led. No independent reading required — activities are shown as images, and the on-screen prompts are written for a grown-up to read aloud. Matching and sorting games use pictures. No timers, no typed responses.
Primary3-5Short independent reading (2-4 sentences), text-based matching games, a light typed response some weeks, and an optional "beat your best time" game mode.

Your student's teacher assigns the lane that matches their grade, but can move a student to the other lane at any point — a younger strong reader into Primary, or an older student who needs more support into Foundations. Tell your teacher if you think a switch would help.

What Your Student Will Learn This Year

  • Quarter 1 — Meeting the Roman World: greetings, the map of Rome, Roman numerals I-X, the founding story of Rome, Roman family life and food, and the first gods of Rome.
  • Quarter 2 — Roman Life & Engineering: more Roman numerals, the Latin roots of our days and months, Roman roads and aqueducts, the Colosseum, more gods, and animal words.
  • Quarter 3 — Roman Words All Around Us: colors, the body, Roman school, weather and seasons, everyday Latin phrases still used today, Pompeii, and the Roman army.
  • Quarter 4 — Rome's Legacy: Roman government words that survive in English, Latin in science, heroes of Roman myth, and a full-year cumulative review.
This course does not teach Latin grammar (noun endings, verb conjugations) — that begins in Middle School Latin. This year builds the vocabulary, cultural knowledge, and curiosity that make that formal grammar work feel familiar rather than brand new.

Materials and Technology

  • No special software — Roman Passport entries are turned in by typing directly into Canvas or uploading a photo of the student's page

Accommodations (IEP / 504)

This course is built with room to accommodate: every game has an untimed mode, every reading passage is short enough for a grown-up to read aloud — and the Foundations prompts are written for exactly that — and reflections can always be given spoken or drawn instead of written. Recorded audio narration is not yet available. If your student has an IEP or 504 plan, contact the teacher at the start of the year so specific arrangements can be made before the first module.

Academic Honesty

At this age, "academic honesty" simply means: let your student do the thinking. It's wonderful to sit with your student during a session, read a passage aloud together, or talk through a matching game — that's exactly how this course is meant to be used. Please don't complete the games or type the reflections for your student; the value is in their own noticing and guessing.
